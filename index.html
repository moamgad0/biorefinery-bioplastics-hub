Circular bioeconomy

Biorefineries and the move away from fossil feedstock

A sustainable supply of energy and materials depends on a bioeconomy built on renewable, bio-based resources. The biorefinery is the technology that makes the circular version of that economy work: an economy powered by nature, running biomass → raw material → processing → technologies → product → recycle.

Three defining characteristics

  • Coupled generation of energy (gaseous or liquid fuels) and materials (chemicals, food, feed).
  • Combined process steps, mechanical (pressing) together with thermochemical (gasification).
  • Diverse raw materials, from both virgin and residual sources.

Biochemical biorefinery against petroleum refinery

Greenhouse gases (CO₂, H₂O, CH₄) absorb the wavelengths a planet radiates, which raises surface temperature. A biorefinery closes the carbon loop because plants and microalgae take the emitted CO₂ back up.

Six biomass sources

Biomass is renewable organic material from land and aquatic crops, agricultural and forestry residues, post-consumer residues, animal and municipal waste, and microorganisms (microalgae, fungi). It should be used in a resource-efficient, energy-efficient and sustainable way.

Plant metabolites

Primary

Involved directly in growth through photosynthesis. Examples: lignin, carbohydrates (starch, cellulose, hemicellulose, sugars), lipids, proteins, nucleic acids.

Together they form lignocellulosic biomass, the most abundant type, which needs pretreatment.

Secondary

Made for defence, in small quantities but with high industrial value. Examples: alkaloids, tannins, terpenoids, phenolics, sterols, steroids, essential oils.

Used in cosmeceuticals, nutraceuticals and pharmaceuticals.

Cell factories

Microalgae, culture collections and metabolic engineering

A cell factory is an engineered microbe or plant cell that makes chemicals, polymers and proteins for the materials and food-ingredient industries. It runs in a closed bioreactor with high efficiency and specificity and a low energy requirement.

Microalgae product families

Most microalgal strains turn large amounts of CO₂ into metabolites that are used directly or as intermediates for fuels and chemicals such as 5-hydroxymethylfurfural (5-HMF), pigments and antioxidants.

Metabolic engineering of E. coli

Metabolic engineering uses experimental, computational and modelling approaches to work out metabolic pathways and change them by genetic, media or other environmental means.

The seven modified pathways

  1. Glucose transport
  2. Central metabolic pathway
  3. Aromatic amino acid pathway
  4. Branch pathway
  5. Degradation pathway
  6. Transport pathway
  7. Pta-AckA pathway (competing acetate route, blocked)

Xylose use in S. cerevisiae

Yeast can be given two heterologous routes from xylose to ethanol.

Microalgal strains

Block competing pathways and overexpress genes for fatty acid and carotenoid synthesis to raise lipid and pigment yield. These are precursors for biodiesel and pharmaceuticals.

Plant alkaloids in E. coli

Copying plant pathways into the cell factory gives high titres of benzylisoquinoline alkaloids, used in pharmaceuticals and nutraceuticals.

Plastic from plants

Bioplastics

A bioplastic is a polymer made into a commercial product from a natural or renewable source. It is positioned as a sustainable alternative to petroplastics.

PetroplasticBioplastic
Global useabout 200 million tons a year, the third most used petroleum derivative2.22 million tonnes of capacity in 2022

Making one

  1. Plant material. Cellulose, collagen, casein, polyester and starch from cassava, legumes, sugarcane and corn.
  2. Dissolution. Conversion to intermediates such as lactic acid and ethanol.
  3. Polymerisation. Bio-based resin, polyethylene and polymer chains.
  4. Forming. Injection moulding into bottles, cutlery and similar products.

Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages

  • Eases dependence on limited oil; no non-renewable raw material.
  • Less non-biodegradable waste; closes the environmental cycle.
  • Buffers petroleum price rises and saves energy in production.
  • Lower greenhouse gas output and carbon footprint.
  • No phthalates or bisphenol, so safe for food packaging.
  • Suits all applications, avoids plastic segregation, helps marketing.

Disadvantages

  • High production cost.
  • Some types need farmland and may compete with food supply.
  • Partly use genetically modified organisms.
  • Shorter lifetime; sensitive to environmental conditions.
  • Poor processability: narrow window (lower melting temperature), brittleness, thermal degradation.
  • Need an additional waste stream and raise compost volume.

Genetically modified bioplastics

Bacteria or yeast are modified to produce enzymes or proteins used to make biodegradable plastics from renewable resources. Making recombinant DNA takes three steps:

  1. Isolate a plasmid from a bacterium and DNA from a donor plant or animal cell.
  2. Cut both with the same restriction enzyme.
  3. Let the sticky ends pair and seal them (ligation) into a recombinant plasmid, then put it in a bacterium that multiplies and clones the gene.
Genetically modified crops are not a technical requirement for any bioplastic sold commercially today. Where they are used, the reason is regional feedstock supply or economics.
